1 Project Overview

What the product is

Skill Passport is an AI interviewer that runs in a web browser. A candidate opens a link and the AI greets them, asks its questions out loud and listens to the spoken answers. It then writes a scored report on how ready the person is for work.

The candidate needs no app and no account, and doesn't have to type. A phone or laptop with a microphone and camera is enough. The interview happens in the language they choose, and the report is in English as well as their language.

The problem it solves

  • Interviews by people don't scale. Trainers can't personally interview hundreds of learners across many centres, and two trainers rarely mark the same way.
  • English is a barrier. Many capable freshers end up judged on their English rather than their skills.
  • Freshers have no work history. Instead of past jobs, Skill Passport asks about everyday life they have already lived, like home, family, travel and festivals.
  • Soft skills are hard to evidence. Each skill gets a score backed by the candidate's own words.

Target users

  • Candidates: freshers on Path Saathi training courses, for example Industrial Electrician.
  • Training and placement staff, who create interviews and review results.
  • The Path Saathi platform, which sends students to the interview and receives their results automatically.

2 Key Objectives & Value

Why it matters

  • The same yardstick for everyone: every candidate is assessed on the same 11 skills against the same scoring guide.
  • Fair to freshers: short answers, nervousness and mixed languages aren't penalised. A sensible "I would do this…" scores well even without work experience.
  • Their language, their pace: questions are spoken in the candidate's language, can be repeated on request, and the interviewer gently checks in if they go quiet.
  • Scale with no extra staff or hand-offs: interviews run on their own at any time, and results flow back to Path Saathi automatically.

What success looks like targets TO CONFIRM

No targets are written down in the project. These measures are suggested and need agreeing with the client.

  • AI scores match trainer judgement. An early comparison with trainers (Nashik) found the AI marking lower, and scoring was adjusted. The acceptable gap still needs agreeing.
  • High completion: most candidates who start an interview finish it without help.
  • Every finished interview reaches Path Saathi with no manual resending.
  • Cost per interview stays within budget. budget TO CONFIRM

3 User Roles

There are four kinds of user. Only staff have accounts. Candidates come in through a link.

Candidate

A fresher being assessed. Needs no account.
  • Opens their link, confirms their details and picks a language
  • Tests microphone and camera, and agrees to be recorded
  • Takes the spoken interview, and can ask for any question to be repeated
  • Can take up to 3 attempts, 15 minutes apart
  • Sees a "submitted, thank you" page. On purpose, they do not see their scores.

Admin (staff)

Training or placement staff, signing in with email and password. TO CONFIRM who holds accounts
  • Creates an interview in one click and shares its link
  • Opens or closes a link to new candidates
  • Follows each candidate's progress
  • Reads reports, plays recordings, compares attempts and downloads PDFs
  • Re-sends a result to Path Saathi or regenerates a report

Path Saathi platform

An automated system connection, not a person.
  • Asks Skill Passport for a personal link for each student
  • Shows the link with the student's details already filled in
  • Receives the full result automatically when the student finishes
  • Can also fetch a result, or its PDF, on request

Project team (internal)

ThreePoint0 Labs. These tools appear only on the team's development copy, never to clients.
  • Sees the AI cost of each interview
  • Re-scores all candidates after a scoring change
  • Compares a candidate's score history and exports it as a PDF
  • Deletes test interviews

4 Core Features

01One-click interview links

"Create interview" gives a shareable link with nothing to configure. Closing a link stops new candidates and keeps existing results.

02Pre-filled start for Path Saathi

Path Saathi students find their name, course and contact details already filled in. Their details travel in an encrypted link.

03Device check and consent

Microphone test, camera preview and a consent box before starting, so problems appear here and not mid-interview.

04Spoken AI interviewer

A natural voice reads each question while the text shows on screen. The candidate just talks, and their words are written down live.

05A gentle warm-up

Easy openers come first: an introduction, hobbies and where they live. They settle nerves and help pick relatable situations later.

0611 workplace skills

Work Readiness, Reliability, Responsibility, Following Instructions, Attention to Detail, Communication, Teamwork, Problem Solving, Adaptability, Initiative and Customer Orientation.

07Questions that fit the person

Everyday situations, not invented office scenes. Questions get gentler if answers are unsure. The customer question uses the candidate's own trade.

08Feels like a conversation

"Repeat" replays a question instantly. After an answer the interviewer asks "anything to add?" and counts down 7 seconds. It checks in after silence and never cuts anyone off.

09Fair, explainable scoring

Scored 0–10 per skill on relevance and reasoning, not length. A strong answer can lift other skills it shows. Calibrated after the comparison with trainers.

10Bilingual reports and PDF

Overall and per-skill scores, a summary, strengths, improvements and every question and answer. In English plus the candidate's language, downloadable as a PDF.

11Fair retakes and resume

Up to 3 attempts, 15 minutes apart, never repeating earlier questions. An interrupted interview picks up where it stopped.

12Integrity safeguards

Camera and voice are recorded privately, leaving the tab is flagged and copying text is discouraged. No website can block screenshots.

13Path Saathi connection

Results go to Path Saathi as soon as a student finishes, with automatic retries. Staff can re-send any that failed.

14Staff dashboard and reports

Totals for interviews, candidates, completions and average score, plus one list of every candidate, searchable by name.

6 How the Interview Works: The Exact Rules

This section answers three questions: what is asked and in what order, how the AI decides each question, and exactly when the interview moves on.

6.1 The order of the interview

Every candidate goes through the same order. Only the wording of the questions changes from person to person.

PartWhat is askedHow the question is chosen
Warm-up (3 parts, not scored)1. "Hello, and welcome! Please tell me your name and a little about yourself."
2. "What do you enjoy doing? Any hobbies or interests?"
3. Where they live.		Fixed wording. Used only to pick situations the candidate can relate to later.
Question 1: Work ReadinessFor example "Why do you want a job?" or "Are you ready to move to another city for a job?"Random pick from a fixed bank of 11, never AI-written. A retake gets one they haven't had.
Questions 2–10Reliability, Responsibility, Following Instructions, Attention to Detail, Communication, Teamwork, Problem Solving, Adaptability, Initiative. One question each, in this order.The AI writes a fresh everyday-life question, modelled on 4 sample questions per skill, e.g. "How do you make sure you reach places on time?"
Question 11: Customer OrientationDealing with an unhappy or confused customer.The one question set in the candidate's own course or trade, e.g. a customer situation from electrical work.
Follow-ups (2 to 3 per interview)One extra question that digs deeper into the same skill.Written by the AI from what the candidate just said. See 6.3 for when they happen.

In total, a candidate hears the 3-part warm-up, 11 skill questions and 2–3 follow-ups.

6.2 How the AI decides what to ask

  • Everyday life, never an invented job: home, family, travel, shopping, festivals, helping friends. "At your job…" or "your manager…" is not allowed.
  • Built on the warm-up: situations lean on the hobbies and places the candidate mentioned. It avoids ones they have no link to, such as cooking for someone who never mentioned cooking.
  • Adjusts to how it's going: after a couple of very short or "I don't know" answers, the next question is simpler and starts with reassurance. If answers are fluent, it keeps a brisk pace.
  • Easy to follow by ear: one question at a time, under 30 words. It uses the candidate's name now and then and may refer back to an earlier answer.
  • Never repeats: not within an interview, and on a retake the AI must choose a different situation from every earlier attempt, not just reword an old question.
  • Off-limits: age, gender, religion, caste, nationality, marriage, family status, disability. It never hints that someone is hired or rejected.
  • No waiting: the next question is prepared while the candidate is still answering.

6.3 When a follow-up question is asked

  • A short answer: if the answer is 6 words or fewer, the AI checks whether a follow-up would help, and asks one if so. A substantive answer goes straight to the next question.
  • "That doesn't apply to me": if the candidate says the situation doesn't fit them ("I don't cook"), the follow-up asks about the same skill in a situation they do relate to. They are not marked down for it.
  • A minimum of 2: most freshers give full answers, so near the end the AI is made to ask follow-ups until at least 2 have been asked.
  • Limits: at most 3 follow-ups per interview and never more than one on the same question. An empty or blank answer never gets one.

6.4 When it moves to the next question

The interview listens all the time. It reacts to silence, but only once the candidate has actually answered, and it never interrupts someone who is still talking. The silence clock is paused while the interviewer itself is speaking.

Every situation, and what the interviewer does

What the candidate doesWhat happens
Answers, then goes quiet for 3 secondsThe interviewer asks "Would you like to add anything?" When that finishes, a 7-second countdown appears on screen. At zero, the answer is saved and the next question plays.
Starts speaking again during the countdownThe countdown stops at once and the new words join the same answer. The next 3-second pause moves on straight away, without asking again.
Says nothing for 8 secondsThe interviewer asks "Did you understand the question, or should I repeat it?" No countdown follows. It just waits, because a countdown here feels like being hurried.
Stays silent for about a minuteThe question is skipped quietly and is not scored. This is a safety net for someone who has walked away from the screen.

Says "I don't know", "I didn't understand" or "skip"The first time, the interviewer gives a short spoken encouragement or explanation. The question stays the same, and the AI never gives away the answer. The second time on the same question, it is skipped and not scored.
Asks "what should I say?"The AI never gives the answer or a hint. The reply is treated as not answering the question.
Presses the Next buttonMoves on at once. Whatever they said is saved as the answer. If they said nothing, the question is skipped.
Talks for 2 minutes without stoppingThe answer is saved automatically at the 2-minute limit and the interview moves on.
Says only 1–2 words, like "okay so"Not yet treated as an answer, because background noise often produces short phantom words like these. They get the 8-second check-in instead of being moved on.
During the warm-upEach of the 3 parts works the same way, including "anything to add?" with the countdown. If they say nothing on a part, the interviewer checks in after 8 seconds and then moves to the next part about 10 seconds later.

7 How the Rating Works

Every skill is rated out of 10. The AI judges each answer, but the final numbers come from fixed arithmetic, the same for every candidate, so they are consistent and can be explained.

7.1 Step by step

1
The answer is written down word for word, in the candidate's own language.
2
The AI scores it 0–10 on the skill the question was about, using the scoring guide in 7.2. It also reads the candidate's earlier answers, so one nervous reply isn't judged on its own.
3
A fixed +1 is added to every score of 5 or above (maximum 10). Below 5 is left alone, so a non-answer is never lifted to a pass. Added after the client found the AI harsher than trainers.
4
Other skills shown in the answer are noted. If an answer about Responsibility also clearly shows good Communication, the AI records that too, with its own score.
5
Each skill gets one score: the average of its main question and any follow-up, plus up to +1 for being shown in other answers (see 7.3). Rounded to a whole number, maximum 10.
6
The overall score is the average of all the skills that were assessed, shown to one decimal place, such as 7.3 / 10. Skipped skills are left out, not counted as zero.
7
The written report: the AI reads all the answers and writes a summary, strengths and areas to improve, but sets no numbers. It is also translated into the candidate's language.

7.2 The scoring guide the AI follows

AI scoreWhat the answer looks likeShown on report (after +1)
9–10Clear, relevant and sensible, plus a specific real example with what they did and what happened10
7–9Clear, relevant answer with sound basic reasoning, even if brief, hypothetical or without an example. A clear answer sits around 7.8–10
5–6Partly answers it, or on the right track but misses part of what was asked6–7
3–4Barely engages with the question, or shows no real reasoning3–4 (no change)
0–2Doesn't answer the question, off-topic, or refuses0–2 (no change)

Never marked down for

  • A short answer that does answer the question
  • "I would do this…" instead of a real past example
  • Ums, pauses, repeating themselves or mixing languages
  • "That situation doesn't apply to me"
  • Not having any work experience

Scored low

  • Not answering what was asked
  • Going off-topic
  • Refusing to answer
  • Asking the AI for the answer
  • No reasoning at all

7.3 Credit across skills

People don't answer one skill at a time. A strong answer can count towards other skills too, but this can only raise a score, never lower it.

  • A skill clearly shown in another answer, scoring 6 or more there, adds +0.5. The most it can add is +1.
  • If a skill was never asked (for example the interview ended early) but was clearly shown in 2 or more other answers, it is scored from those. With only one sighting it shows as "not assessed".
  • Saying "I am responsible" is a claim and doesn't count. Describing what they actually did does.

7.4 A worked example

SkillWhat happenedSkill score
ReliabilityAI scores the answer 7, +1 → 88
CommunicationMain answer 6 → 7. Follow-up 8 → 9. Average of 7 and 98
TeamworkAI scores 4. Below 5, so no +14

Not scored: the warm-up, "repeat" requests and skipped questions. Re-scoring: if the scoring rules improve, past interviews can be re-graded from the stored answers. A re-score can only raise a score, and every earlier version is kept.

The parts, in plain English

  • The website and coordinator (server): the screens people use in their browser, with nothing to install. Behind them, the server runs each interview step by step, enforces the retake rules and controls who can see what.
  • The ears and voice (Sarvam): an Indian AI provider. It speaks the questions, writes down answers live, writes questions and scores answers.
  • The backup brain (OpenAI): takes over question writing and scoring automatically if Sarvam is slow or failing.
  • The memory (database): interviews, candidates, every question and answer, scores and report history.
  • The archive (private storage): audio and video recordings. Staff playback uses links that expire after 5 minutes.
  • The printer (PDF maker): "prints" the real report page, so the PDF always matches the screen.

12 Glossary

AI model
Software trained on large amounts of text that can write questions and judge answers.
API
A doorway that lets one system talk to another automatically, for example Path Saathi asking for a link.
API key
A secret password one system uses to prove who it is to another.
Attempt
One sitting of an interview by one candidate. A candidate can have up to 3.
Backup provider (fallback)
A second AI service used automatically when the main one fails.
Cloud storage
Files kept on secure internet servers instead of a local computer.
Database
An organised store of all the product's records.
Encrypted link
A link whose hidden contents only our server can read.
Interview link
The web address candidates open to start. It is hard to guess, so only people sent it can use it.
Live speech relay
A small helper service that passes the candidate's voice to the listening service in real time.
Production / staging
Production is the live site for real users. Staging is a copy used for testing.
Re-score
Grading stored answers again after the scoring rules change. Here, scores can only go up.
Scoring guide (rubric)
The written rules that say what a 3, a 7 or a 10 looks like.
Server
A computer on the internet that runs the application for everyone.
Speech-to-text
Turning spoken words into written text.
Text-to-speech
Turning written text into a spoken voice.
Transcript
The written record of what the candidate said.
Webhook
An automatic message one system sends another when something happens, such as "this student finished".
